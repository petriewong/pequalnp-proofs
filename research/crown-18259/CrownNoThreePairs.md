# A 22-gate Crown7 circuit has at most two pure matched ORs

The preceding bounds exclude four or more pure pairs and the three-pair case without repeated raws. This closes the three-pair case with repeated raws. It does not exclude a 22-gate circuit with fewer pure pairs.

Use the definitions and safeguards in CrownRepeatReaders.md. After the four earlier filters, the 11 necessary repeated-input profiles have exactly four or five O raws; no profile has zero. All original ANDs number at most six, all O feeders have clean companions, and the pure matched ORs are the only conduits of their raw inputs. Here clean means false on every original matched diagonal.

## A normalized circuit of at most 22 gates would have no O readers

Erase all reads of original clean outputs inside the circuit, and collect the productive original clean ANDs with a new terminal OR tree. Preserving the promised table by this transformation is the first-true clean gate and terminal filtering argument in CleanErase.lean and CleanAndTerminal.lean. Clean ORs contribute nothing, and each original O feeder simplifies to its original raw input. The O feeders are distinct: a shared sole reader on different labels masks a diagonal pivot, and sole matched OR would be a pure pair, not a split feeder.

Any dirty original gate that remains in the routing still accepts some diagonal. On every original diagonal all earlier clean values were already zero, so erasure changes none of its diagonal evaluations. Subsequent constant simplification and redirecting aliases preserve surviving signals. Thus all usable signals in the new interior routing are dirty: raws, pure ORs, and the remaining original dirty gates. No nonzero clean signal can feed an interior gate; the new terminal-only OR tree reads the clean products, never raw inputs. In particular, no sole reader of a split raw can be an OR in that tree (a raw would make the final OR accept its own diagonal).

All three pure ORs are still retained after useful simplification: their two raw inputs are essential and still appear only there. If the transformed circuit used at most 22 gates, it would still have exactly three pure pairs (four or more excluded), at least one repeated raw (the no-repeat case is excluded), and a surviving necessary profile. Every O reader in such a profile has a clean companion by a saturated cut avoiding its tied label; but its only possible OR companion is an interior dirty signal. No O readers are therefore possible. This contradicts the 11-profile list, whose members all have four or five O readers. The contradiction applies even if the transformed circuit has a different raw-reader profile from its source.

## The potential one-gate overhead is impossible

If there are five O feeders, or if at most five clean AND terms survive, the above transformation is already non-increasing in size: there are at most six terms, so the number of added ORs is at most five, and when there are only four O drops and at most five terms the OR cost is at most four. Consequently both cases are excluded.

The sole remaining scenario is exactly four O feeders, six surviving clean AND terms, and no additional original gate removed: a further removal would pay the fifth added OR and lead to the excluded at-most-22 normalized circuit. All six original ANDs must then be clean and productive. The original output must be one of those ANDs: a clean OR output would be another removed gate, distinct from all four dirty O feeders. All five nonfinal clean ANDs are live in the original circuit. None can read directly into another retained AND, because erasing its clean output would make that AND zero and leave at most five productive terms. Every direct OR consumer of these five gates must be among the four removed O feeders (otherwise that OR would simplify as an additional removed gate). Since each O feeder has an original raw input, it has room for only one such AND. Five distinct nonfinal ANDs cannot each have a consumer among four one-slot feeders. Contradiction.

## Independent checks in three subcases

For AO,AO,RO,RO the five mandatory drops and at most-five terminal ORs would leave exactly six clean ANDs and eight routing ORs if a 22 circuit survived. The two repeated left raws have four reader incidences and at most one shared primitive OR (duplicate gates are removable). Setting both to zero to measure the two A star supports would thus remove at least three routing ORs. At most five remain, below the known six needed to compute the two all-but-one star unions on seven opposite atoms.

For AO,RO,RO,RO and AR,OO,RO,RO, the same six-clean/eight-routing situation leaves at least three routing ORs deleted by zeroing the three repeated raw inputs. A single A star's companion requires a union of six opposite atoms, needing at least five surviving ORs. Consequently the three repeated raws must use exactly three raw-reading gates, forming the triangle of pair-ORs; all five other ORs are consumed by a binary tree over the star's six atoms. In the second profile the repeated right raw is required to share one OR with each mismatched repeated left raw, forcing that same triangle. No other raw can appear in the six-atom star routing: setting it to zero would drop an extra OR, contradicting the five-OR floor. A remaining output term can only choose from raw sources not already committed to an exclusive reader, the three triangle ORs, and the five outputs of the star union tree. Each of the 945 labeled full binary union trees was enumerated, and neither profile can cover the remaining positive rows by at most five clean products. The separate top-down exact coverage and bottom-up/Z3 scripts agree. These checks are independent follow-ups for the scoped profiles, not the basis for the general pigeonhole argument.
