# Two pure matched ORs at 22 gates require at least four exceptional split raws

Assume exactly two pure matched pairs, at most 22 gates, and exactly three exceptional split raws. Ordinary means a raw with exactly one OR reader. Call an ordinary feeder dirty when its companion accepts its own diagonal; its companion cannot accept any other diagonal. Two such feeders cannot be dirty at the same label, because each companion would need a route through the other's sole reader before itself.

## At most one dirty ordinary feeder

Suppose there are two dirty ordinary labels i,j. For any exceptional raw r outside those two labels, if a mismatched opposite ordinary raw t also lies outside i,j, cut through r,t. At least three elementary gates outside the pair prefix are lost. Indeed, a two-gate exceptional shadow has types A or R and is disjoint from the mismatched ordinary feeder by masking, as before; a larger shadow supplies three losses itself. The two retained dirty feeders are distinct from these losses, since on each own retained diagonal both its raw and its companion are true. Their companions absorb their ordinary raw after twins are tied, giving two additional aliases. Together with the two pure pair losses this exceeds six. Thus any such r,t is impossible when there are two dirty labels.

Pick a side with at least two exceptional raws. For any exception on this side, of the four differently labeled opposite choices at most one is exceptional; even avoiding i,j leaves an ordinary choice. Hence all exceptions on this side must have labels in {i,j}. There cannot be three on that side, because they have three distinct labels. If there are two, their labels are exactly i,j. The third exception is opposite and cannot share either label, since then both raws at that label would be exceptional and no ordinary dirty feeder could be there. For that third exception, choosing a differently labeled opposite ordinary raw also avoids i,j: the only exceptions on that opposite side are at i,j themselves, and at least two other choices remain. This is again a contradiction. There are always at least six clean ordinary feeders.

## Limits for OR-reader exceptions

An exceptional raw with only OR readers has exactly two or three distinct OR readers. Four cannot fit: if an elementary shadow had four gates, all four opposite raw inputs at differently labeled split pairs must have all their readers' operand slots in those four gates. Each shadow gate already needs one input from the removed region, so each opposite raw has exactly one reader and one of them shares a direct reader of the exception. A shared AND makes it inoperative on positives not using that exception; a shared OR masks its pivot on the exception's diagonal. Both are impossible. In the all-OR-reader case the elementary shadow has just the direct OR readers, since an OR with the exceptional raw and any distinct companion is not forced zero by eliminating that raw alone.

Three OR readers already give an impossible cut: choose any differently labeled opposite ordinary raw (at least two choices); its feeder cannot directly read the exception by masking. The two pure prefix ORs, the three OR readers, and the ordinary feeder give six OR losses. The ten-OR projected floor forces at most six original ANDs; at least six clean ordinary feeders can be erased, and at most five terminal ORs are needed. This reaches the impossible terminalized at-most-21 case.

If an exception r has two OR readers, a cut through r and a differently labeled ordinary opposite raw always has five OR losses. If none of the seven ordinary feeders is dirty, at most seven ANDs remain and erasing all seven ordinary feeders suffices. If there is one dirty ordinary feeder at a label different from r, choose the opposite ordinary cut away from that label (four choices minus at most two exceptional raws and the retained label). Its dirty feeder survives the five primitive losses and aliases on tying, giving a sixth OR loss. So at most six ANDs and at least six erasures again suffice.

Consequently any OR-reader exception in this three-exception branch must have exactly two OR readers and be at the unique dirty feeder's label. That label must contain its ordinary mate, so at most one exceptional raw is there. In particular the other two exceptions have at least one direct AND reader. The three-exception cases with AND readers, larger exception sets, and fewer pure pairs remain open.

## The subcase where all three shadows have size two is impossible

The two-gate shadow classifications are A (a sole AND H then sole OR K) and R (two ORs). We have at most one R. An A's ordinary mate, if present, is clean by the earlier singleton argument. An A's H is automatically clean on all other diagonals because it reads its raw by AND. An A's K cannot coincide with any mismatched ordinary feeder: setting that ordinary raw high masks the A raw's entire downstream route at that diagonal. A K that is the ordinary mate feeder has the form H OR twin, so aliases the twin after tying regardless of H's value.

If all three exceptions are A, they must all lie on the same side. Any split of these three across the sides includes a mismatched opposite pair, which cannot activate either sole AND at their prescribed positive (the earliest reader has a false companion before either raw can propagate). A cut through one A and a differently labeled ordinary opposite raw removes five distinct gates, at least four of them OR, so there are at most eight original ANDs.
* If an ordinary feeder is dirty, its label is not any A label. For each A at label i, choose the five-loss cut through another A, avoiding i and the dirty label. One loss is already added by the retained dirty ordinary feeder after tying. A true H on its own diagonal or a K identical to its mate O would then add a seventh distinct loss. The H survives if true (its retained diagonal shows it); the mate K is distinct from all designated cut losses by its form and aliases either by H becoming zero or by absorption on tying. Thus each H is clean and every K distinct from all ordinary feeders. Three Hs need at least two different K gates, so the six clean ordinary feeders and at least two extra collapses pay for terminalization.
* If there is no dirty ordinary feeder, seven clean ordinary feeders are available. There cannot be two Hs that accept their own diagonal, because a five-loss cut through the third A avoiding both labels would add their two aliases. Hence at least two Hs are clean. If all clean Hs' K gates were their own mate O feeders, choose two and a five-loss cut through the third A avoiding their labels. Both distinct mate Ks alias by absorption (or earlier raw elimination), again adding two losses. At least one clean H therefore gives a separate K collapse. Eight drops suffice.

If there are two As and one R, the R is at the unique dirty ordinary mate label, distinct from both A raws. Any R-plus-ordinary cut loses five ORs, so there are at most seven ANDs. Six ordinary feeders are clean, and one further K collapse suffices. If the As share a label, both Hs reject their own diagonal by the earliest-reader argument. Neither K can coincide with a matched (none exists) or mismatched ordinary feeder. If the As are at different labels, keep one A label and the dirty R label while cutting the other A with an opposite ordinary raw. There is an ordinary choice: the other two exceptions are both on avoided labels, so they exclude no additional labels. As above the retained dirty O occupies loss six, forcing the retained H to be clean and its K distinct from its mate's ordinary feeder, yielding the additional collapse.

All cases reach the impossible terminalized size at most 21. Thus exactly three exceptional split raws require at least one size-three elementary shadow. We do not exclude that remaining case or the unrestricted 22-gate target.

## A size-three shadow cannot contain only one OR

Suppose an exception r has a three-gate shadow D with only one OR K. If K directly reads r, the forced region with two ANDs can be removed (r=0 gives zeros; r=1 gives K=1), leaving at most 20 gates and two original pure pairs, impossible independently of the number of exceptions. Thus no direct OR reads r. The single OR K is the sole boundary of the forced region, and cannot be an incompatible ordinary feeder: such a feeder reading t directly would mask r's entire effect with t high on t's diagonal. Thus a cut through r and a mismatched opposite ordinary raw always loses six gates. It can avoid any one specified label different from r's: of four compatible opposite choices, remove at most two exceptions and the retained label. This forces every ordinary feeder away from r to have a clean companion. The ordinary mate of r, if present, is clean too by the singleton r argument, because r is read only by ANDs. All seven ordinary feeders are clean.

If any exception has at least two ORs in its shadow, a cut through it and a mismatched opposite ordinary feeder outside its shadow gives five OR losses and hence at most seven ANDs. Such a feeder always exists. There are at least two compatible opposite ordinary choices. At size two the shadow is disjoint from them by masking; at size three it can overlap at most one, by the two-overlap earliest-reader argument in CrownTwoPairsTwoExceptions.md (that local overlap proof uses only that the two overlapping feeders are ordinary, not the total number of exceptions). Thus seven ordinary erasures suffice in this case.

Otherwise every exception's shadow has exactly one OR, and each exceptional raw has a sole direct AND reader. This is already proved for size two. At size three there are two forced ANDs and only one boundary OR: if both ANDs directly read r, neither can read the other (it would be r AND a gate that already implies r), since the redundant gate would give at most 21 and the redundancy can instead be treated below. If the two direct ANDs are not related by an edge, every path from them to the unforced output would have to go directly through K, which cannot read both because it must have one unforced input. Thus the two forced ANDs form a chain from a sole direct reader. If an AND in the chain redundantly also reads r, simplify that AND; in a candidate with at most 21 gates, elementary zero-shadows have size at most two, retaining both pure pairs. More directly, the apparent pair of direct AND readers in the original circuit then still behaves exactly as the single initial AND on all inputs, so in the argument below they have the same activation obstruction. No independent information from r is gained by this redundant repeated read.

All three exceptions must be on the same side. Otherwise an opposite mismatched pair would have each its first effective AND reading only its own raw and a companion before the other raw's first effective reader. On the prescribed positive pair the first such AND is false and then the second is false as well. A shared initial AND cannot be their only effective route, since then neither can influence its required positives with other labels. This is the same earliest-reader obstruction as for A–A.

Choose the saturated six-loss cut through r and an opposite ordinary raw while retaining another exception's label i. If the last forced AND of that exception accepted its own diagonal, it would survive the six losses and alias its raw in the tied cube; this is impossible. It is clean on the other diagonals because forced ANDs imply their exceptional raw. Its sole boundary OR K_i cannot be an ordinary feeder on another label by the unique-boundary masking argument. Nor can it be its ordinary mate feeder, as then it would alias the mate by absorption on tying, giving a seventh loss distinct from the designated six (or collapse before tying if the forced AND becomes zero). Thus a clean AND erasure collapses a K_i distinct from the seven ordinary feeders. The saturated cut has at least four OR losses, leaving at most eight ANDs originally, so eight drops suffice. This excludes every one-OR three-gate shadow in this branch, regardless of the remaining shadow placements.

## Residual shape: one mixed reader and two sole AND readers

A three-gate shadow now has exactly two ORs and one AND: three direct OR readers are already excluded; with no AND all its OR losses are direct. If the sole AND H is the only direct reader, its exits must be the two shadow ORs. It cannot overlap a compatible opposite ordinary feeder G = H OR t, since then on the prescribed pair r,t the companion of r at H cannot be true: t only enters at downstream G and r is read only at H. On that pair H would be just as false as on singleton t, masking r. Thus saturated cuts through r and compatible opposite ordinary raws can retain any other label and force those feeders clean. The ordinary mate of r is also clean by the singleton argument. The cut gives at least five OR losses, so seven clean feeder erasures with at most seven ANDs contradict the terminalized bound.

If r has two direct OR readers as well as the direct AND H, all exits from H are through the two direct ORs. They are both true on r=1 and H is false on r=0, so remove H. This leaves at most 21 gates and cannot make any ordinary raw exceptional: if a direct OR is changed at all, its other input was H and it aliases r, and in this case it could not have also read an ordinary raw. There are then at most three exceptions and, by the three-loss outside-prefix budget at size 21, all shadows have size at most two. The zero-, one-, two-, or all-size-two three-exception exclusions apply. So every surviving size-three shadow is type M: one direct OR G, one direct AND H, and one OR K that is the boundary of H. K is not G (otherwise H's route is redundant and removal with the same argument suffices). There is no other consumer of the forced AND H.

Each M has access to a saturated cut with at least five OR losses: among at least two compatible ordinary opposite feeders, at most one overlaps its shadow. This yields at most seven ANDs. Hence there must be one dirty ordinary label j, or all seven ordinary feeders erase and exclude the candidate. For any M r away from j, every compatible ordinary opposite cut choice avoiding j must overlap D; otherwise a saturated cut retains j and its dirty feeder aliases for loss seven. There is at least one such choice and at most one can overlap, so there is exactly one. This is possible only when the two other exceptions are on the opposite side at distinct labels (not r's label), and j is a further distinct label not shared with any of the three exceptions. In that situation those other exceptions each have at most one exception on the opposite side, so they cannot be M away from j, nor can either occupy j. Therefore if any M is away from j, it is the only M. If all Ms are at j, there can only be one, because j also has an ordinary mate.

No two-OR exception R is possible: R must be at j. If M were at j too, no ordinary dirty feeder could remain on that label; if M is elsewhere, we just proved j cannot be any exceptional label. All non-M exceptions are therefore the two-gate type A. We reduce to exactly one M and two As, with precisely one dirty ordinary label:
* M is at the dirty label; the two As are either on the same side or opposite at a matched label.
* M is away from the dirty label, the two As are on the opposite side from M, and all three exceptional labels and the dirty label are distinct. The one eligible opposite ordinary feeder at label other than the dirty label is the OR boundary K of M's forced AND H. (The direct OR G cannot read that ordinary raw by masking.)

This is a reduction of the exactly-three-exception slice only, not a claim about larger exception counts or unrestricted size 22.

## Both mixed-reader placements are excluded

The M above has a label distinct from each A (in the first placement it has an ordinary mate; in the second the three labels are distinct). Choose either A at label i. Among four compatible labels for an opposite cut through M, avoid i and all exceptional raws on the opposite side. At most two labels are excluded: if both As are opposite, i is already among their two labels, while if at least one A lies on M's side, at most one exceptional label is opposite. At least two ordinary choices remain. As at most one choice overlaps the three-gate M shadow, a cut avoiding i with six distinct elementary losses always exists.

That cut forces the retained A's H clean. Otherwise H accepts its own original diagonal, survives the raw deletion, and aliases its retained raw after tying, losing a forbidden seventh gate. The H cannot accept any other diagonal because it implies its own raw. The retained A's K cannot be an ordinary feeder on a mismatched label by masking, or its mate feeder: in the latter case K = H OR twin would alias the retained raw after tying (or disappear during raw deletion if H became zero). This additional loss would be distinct from the six: K contains the retained H and its mate raw, so is neither a direct M reader nor the chosen ordinary feeder; it is not the M shadow exit because that exit would have to read M's forced AND, which is distinct from the retained H. The two Hs are distinct since sharing the M's H as the A's sole reader would suppress every required positive involving that A raw when M's raw is absent. Thus the retained K is not an ordinary feeder.

The clean H therefore gives a separate K deletion, in addition to the six clean ordinary feeders. We had at most seven original ANDs, so adding at most six terminal ORs yields at most 21 gates. This is impossible. Consequently any at-most-22 Crown7 circuit with exactly two pure matched ORs needs **at least four exceptional split raws**. The complete unrestricted 22-gate question remains open.
