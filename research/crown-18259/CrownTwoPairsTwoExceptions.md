# Two exceptional raws at 22 gates: both zero-shadows have size two

Assume exactly two pure matched ORs, at most 22 gates, and exactly two exceptional split raws. An ordinary raw has a sole OR reader. The cases with zero or one exception are already excluded. We exclude any such candidate with an exceptional shadow of at least three gates.

## Shadow size and overlap

As in CrownTwoPairsOneException.md, for an exceptional raw r its elementary zero-shadow D (zero gates plus aliasing ORs) has from two to four gates, using the opposite-cut threshold-two floor and the two independent pure-prefix losses. Four is impossible. If four gates are lost, the entire outside-prefix budget is exhausted; each of four opposite raws at different split labels must have all its readers in D or its deletion would contribute another gate. Each shadow gate already needs an operand supplied by r or its forced-zero region, leaving one slot. Thus each of the four opposite raws appears exactly once, including one in a direct reader of r. If that shared reader is AND, the other raw can have no influence when r is zero, contradicting its positives with another label. If OR, turning on that other raw at r's own diagonal is masked, again contradicting a positive off-diagonal subset. So d <= 3.

Suppose D has three gates. At most one of the four compatible opposite ordinary feeders can lie in D. Such a feeder cannot directly read r by masking. If two were in D, the remaining gate H would be r's only direct reader and must be AND (r is exceptional). Neither of the overlapping ordinary ORs can be forced zero, since each reads a surviving raw; consequently both read H as their forced-zero operand. Pick one of their raws t. The companion of r at H is independent of both r and t: their only readers are H itself and a gate strictly downstream of H, respectively. On the promised positive pair r,t, H is zero. With the same t and r off it is also zero; r therefore cannot toggle the output from the false singleton t to the required true pair. This is impossible.

## Saturated cuts force clean feeders

Pick an ordinary feeder at a label different from r. Among four opposite labels at different indices, avoid the feeder's retained label and the potential other exception. At least two ordinary cut choices remain. At most one overlaps D, so choose outside D. This cut already loses six gates (two prefix, three shadow, one chosen feeder). A retained ordinary feeder cannot have a companion that accepts any other diagonal, by masking. If its companion accepts its own diagonal, both operands are true on an assignment where the cut raws are zero, so the feeder is not among these six losses. Then under twin identification it aliases its companion on the retained tied cube; this would be loss seven. Therefore each ordinary feeder away from r's label has a clean companion. At least seven of the eight ordinary feeders are away from r's label.

If D has at least two ORs, a saturated cut loses at least five ORs, and hence there were at least 15 ORs and at most seven ANDs. Erase at least seven clean-companion feeders and add at most six terminal ORs, obtaining at most 21 gates.

If D has only one OR, there was at least one lost OR by the zero-region boundary argument. If that OR directly reads r, it is the entire boundary; the forced region can be deleted exactly as in CrownTwoPairsOneException.md. Ordinary feeders stay ordinary, and both original essential pure matched gates survive. More explicitly for this three-gate case: if G reads both r and the forced region, it aliases r; all other removed gates are forced-zero ANDs. The three-gate shadow then has at least two ANDs. In any resulting candidate at most 20 gates remain, and with two pure pairs the outside-prefix cut budget is at most two. A size-two individual shadow exhausts this budget, but its two remaining input slots cannot hold all four opposite compatible split raws. Therefore every split raw is ordinary in such a candidate, already excluded; an extra pure pair is likewise already excluded. Thus in the one-shadow-OR case, all direct readers of r are ANDs. On r alone all earlier nonraw gates are false. This makes the O feeder on r's own label, if any, clean as in CrownTwoPairsOneException.md. All eight ordinary feeders are now clean, and the saturated cut loses at least four ORs (hence there were at most eight ANDs). Erase eight feeders and add at most seven terminal ORs.

Both alternatives obtain a terminalized circuit with at most 21 gates and two pure pairs, or form a third pure pair, both excluded by prior results. This excludes every size-three shadow in the two-exception branch; the remaining options at each exception are one direct AND followed by a sole OR, or two direct OR readers. Neither the remaining two-exception cases nor the unrestricted 22-gate problem are decided here.
