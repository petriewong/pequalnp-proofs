# Two partial input deletions strengthen the raw Crown gate budget

A pure pair gate is a physical OR on x_i,y_i whose two raw inputs occur nowhere else. For every monotone raw circuit representing the seven-index Crown table, identify surviving x_i,y_i with a logical variable z_i after setting x_a=0 and y_b=0 for arbitrary distinct indices a,b. The result is threshold two on z. For a singleton its raw assignment is below the false diagonal. For a distinct pair of z flags, at least one off-diagonal orientation survives the opposite deletions; this makes the result true by monotonicity. The singleton/pair conditions determine all assignments, including zero. CrownPartialIdentification.lean checks the generic first two layers and survival condition.

The already derived minimum size of seven-variable monotone threshold two is 16 binary gates. Thus any 22-gate raw candidate loses at most six gates on every such projection.

Let p be the number of pure pair gates. If the two deletions are in *unpaired* indices, all p pure gates disappear independently of any constant propagation through the unpaired input wires (each becomes z_i OR z_i).
* For p=6, omit one raw side of the sole remaining unpaired index, identify everything else, and keep all pairs positive. That one-sided restriction removes at least one additional gate because that raw was an essential input in the original circuit; the total exceeds six. The p=7 case is already excluded by full pairing.
* For p≤5, let u=7−p. If x_a is an unpaired left input, let D consist of gates that have a persistently zero operand after x_a=0 and forward structural zero propagation. A binary monotone gate touched by zero simplifies even if its output is not zero. Every D gate has an input from x_a or from an internally certified zero. The same argument works with left/right swapped. For each of the u−1 possible unpaired right partners y_b at b≠a, every direct consumer of y_b must disappear on the joint projection, and distinct disappearing gates outside the pure prefix have budget at most 6−p=u−1. In particular |D|≤u−1.
* Equality |D|=u−1 is impossible. Otherwise every direct consumer of each of those u−1 right raws is in D. Each such gate has only one operand slot not already occupied by a zero-origin wire. Hence each right raw consumes exactly one distinct slot, fills all slots, and has exactly one reader. Some gate in D reads x_a directly because x_a is essential. Its other operand is then a right y_b of differing index that only reads through this gate. This is impossible: an OR with x_a masks the dependence on y_b when only x_a is on; an AND masks it when a third left x_c (c!=a,b) is the only left input. In both cases turning y_b on must yield a Crown edge. Thus every unpaired raw's zero-shadow D contains at most u−2 gates.
* For p=5, u=2 gives no budget even for a single raw reader. Thus a 22-gate candidate has at most four pure pairs. For p=4, each of the six unpaired raws has exactly one reader, and it cannot be AND: its zero output would force a second gate to simplify, while it cannot be the final gate since there are true rows without that raw. Therefore all fourteen primaries have a single reader, every raw-reading gate is OR, and six particular OR gates disappear in each unpaired two-deletion projection (the four pairs and two now-zero consumers). Logical seven-variable threshold two needs at least ten ORs, so any such 22-gate candidate has at least sixteen raw ORs and at most six ANDs.

These are structural follow-on arguments, not an upstream certificate of the full raw question. The independent audit script checks all forty-two choices on ten existing validated Crown circuits; it is a regression test of the transformations, not a generic lower-bound proof.
