# Two size-two exceptions: only two repeated inputs on distinct labels need remain

Assume two pure matched ORs and exactly two exceptional split raws at size at most 22. By the preceding shadow reduction, an exceptional raw is either A (sole AND H, with sole OR consumer K) or R (two OR readers).

Each cut through an exception r and a compatible opposite ordinary raw loses five gates: two pure prefix gates, the two shadow gates of r, and the ordinary feeder. The last is separate by masking: a shared OR would be the sole reader of the ordinary raw (if it directly read r, that raw would be masked on r's diagonal); if shared with an A's downstream K, it would mask A on the ordinary raw's diagonal. One may always pick such a cut avoiding up to two specified split labels other than r's label, since there are four compatible opposite labels and at most one other exception. If r is R, the cut loses at least five ORs, so there are at most seven ANDs; otherwise the lost OR count is at least four, so there are at most eight ANDs.

For every exception r, at most one ordinary feeder at a label other than r's is own-dirty. If two distinct ordinary labels were own-dirty, choose the above cut avoiding both. Both feeders then survive the elementary losses (they accept their own raw diagonal through both operands), and both alias their companions after tying, exceeding six losses. Own-dirty ordinary feeders cannot share a label by acyclicity. Thus any two dirty ordinary feeders must be at the two distinct exception labels. If one exception is A, its ordinary mate (if present) is clean by the singleton argument; and if the exceptions share a label, that label has no ordinary mate.

- An A–R pair, or R–R exceptions sharing a label, admits at least seven clean ordinary feeders and at most seven original ANDs. Clean erasure pays for terminalization at most 21.
- Two opposite mismatched A raws are impossible. The earlier H is false at the required positive on those two raws: its other operand is independent of both raws, since their sole readers are the two Hs. The later H, if any, also remains false by topological order. Thus adding the second raw to a false singleton cannot change the output.
- Two matched A raws have both Hs clean, by the same earliest-reader argument on their own diagonal and by the raw AND input on every other diagonal. There is at most one dirty ordinary feeder. A K cannot be an ordinary feeder on another label, because K as a direct reader of that raw would mask the corresponding A raw when turned on at the ordinary raw's diagonal. There is no ordinary raw on the matched label, so erasing a clean H collapses at least one K distinct from all ordinary feeders. At least eight drops pay for at most seven terminal ORs.
- Two same-side A raws have clean ordinary mates, unless that mate is not ordinary (which cannot occur with both exceptions on the same side). If no ordinary feeder is dirty, erase eight feeders. If one is dirty, it has label j distinct from both exceptions. Choose one of the exceptions r and a five-loss cut through r avoiding j and the other exceptional label i. The retained dirty ordinary feeder aliases for loss six. The other exception's H must be clean: if H is true on its own diagonal, it aliases its raw in the tied projection for loss seven. And its K cannot be its own twin's ordinary feeder: such a K is H OR twin, so on tying label i it aliases the twin independently of H's cleanliness. This K is distinct from the five designated losses (neither r-shadow gate by mismatched masking, nor the chosen O feeder since its own label is retained). It aliases either during raw simplification if H becomes zero, or during tying by absorption. Either way it is a seventh distinct alias together with the dirty feeder, impossible. A mismatched K sharing an ordinary feeder is already forbidden by masking. Thus the clean H supplies an extra distinct K collapse, giving at least eight drops.
- Opposite mismatched R–R with disjoint shadows loses six ORs on their joint cut, so there are at most six ANDs. This saturated cut forces every ordinary feeder away from the two exception labels to be clean. There are at least six such feeders, paying for at most five terminal ORs.

Every resulting terminalized circuit is excluded at size 21. Thus a surviving exactly-two-exception candidate has two R raws at distinct labels, both their ordinary mates own-dirty (otherwise seven clean ordinary feeders suffice), and if the exceptions are opposite they share one direct OR. The same-side and shared-opposite residuals are not settled here.
