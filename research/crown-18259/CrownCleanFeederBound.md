# Clean feeder erasure excludes four pure pairs in a 22-gate Crown circuit

A pure pair is a matched OR whose two original raw inputs appear nowhere else. See CrownPartialBound.md for opposite-coordinate deletion and the logical 16-gate lower bound. This argument uses the exact promise consisting of 49 row/column inputs.

## Clean-terminal normal form

Tag a wire clean if it rejects all seven diagonals on the original circuit. No raw wire is clean. Compute the entire circuit with clean signals replaced by zero on every *internal read*, but collect each clean AND's calculated output and OR all those collected values at the end. This preserves the Crown truth table. On a true promised row, the earliest true clean gate exists (the original output is clean), and the erasure cannot affect any prior value because all earlier clean values were already zero on that row. The earliest true clean gate cannot be an OR: a clean OR must have two clean operands, one of which would have been true earlier. On a diagonal, monotonicity ensures erasure cannot make any previously false collected value true. CleanErase.lean verifies preservation of the collective OR under clean-input erasure. CleanAndTerminal.lean verifies that the erased clean ORs contribute nothing and verifies small numerical budgets. Physical rewiring and gate counting below are separate.

Call a split input a clean feeder if its sole reader has the form x_i OR H with H clean. That feeder is dirty (it accepts its own diagonal), but it becomes just x_i in the erased circuit and its gate disappears. Every pure pair is dirty and remains: both raw inputs have no other users and the new output still depends on them, so no dead-code pruning can discard their sole joint signal. If f such feeders disappear and the old circuit has at most a AND gates, the new circuit uses at most k−f+max(a−1,0) gates, counting the final OR tree. Constant remnants can be propagated away without adding gates.

## Four pure pairs

The partial-deletion budget forces every split raw to be read exactly once through an OR and at most six AND gates in the entire circuit. For a split raw x_i with sole reader x_i OR H, H rejects every other diagonal: at such a row, toggling x_i must create a true off-diagonal, and a true H would mask it. H also rejects its own diagonal in this architecture. After co-deleting opposite raws on the other two split labels, both raws of i remain tied. If H accepted singleton z_i, monotonicity would imply H >= z_i and the feeder would be an additional redundant gate. The four pair gates and two co-deleted raw readers already exhaust the six allowed losses; this extra removal contradicts the logical 16-floor. Hence all six split raws are clean feeders. Their erasure pays for terminalization and at least one gate is saved (6 disappear, at most 5 added), leaving at most 21 gates while preserving the four pairs. But any such circuit loses at least four pair gates and two distinct split readers on the partial projection, so must have at least 22 gates. Contradiction. Combined with prior p >= 5 exclusion, at most three pure pairs remain possible.

## Three pure pairs at 21 or fewer

There are four split labels and only two raw gate removals available after the three pure gates. If any split raw had a zero-shadow containing two gates (a zero-shadow counts all original gates with some forced-zero operand upon killing that one raw), each of its three mismatched opposite split partners would have to place all readers inside those two gates. Each gate has at most one slot for these raws, so this is impossible. Thus every split raw has a single reader, an OR (a raw AND would turn its output zero and force a further gate simplification, and can't itself be the output). Co-deleting opposite raws on two different split labels collapses two distinct ORs, in addition to the three pure ORs. Each split OR feeder must have a clean companion, including on its own diagonal: co-delete on two other labels and keep its own label tied; all five permitted losses are already spent. There are at most six AND gates (five eliminated ORs plus the ten-OR logical lower bound). Erasing eight feeders and adding at most five ORs yields at most 18 gates with all three pure pairs. This contradicts the minimum 21 needed just to cover the core 16 and five forced removals. Thus three pure pairs require at least 22 gates.

## Some of the three-pair footprints at 22

With no repeated raw and all split readers OR, each companion is clean except possibly on its own diagonal. A given split label cannot have two own-diagonal-accepting companions, since each requires an earlier path through the other's sole reader. Two different labels cannot have such dirty companions either: co-deleting opposite raws in the other two split labels would remove three pair gates, two deleted readers and these two tied-duplicate feeders, leaving fewer than 16. Hence at least seven clean feeders. Co-deletion removes at least five ORs, so AND count is at most seven. Terminalization saves a gate, contradicting the three-pair 21 exclusion.

With no repeated raw and precisely one split raw AND reader A, its zero-shadow is two gates (A and its unique downstream consumer B). B is OR: if B were AND it would become zero and have a further downstream gate, and B cannot itself compute the output with A as its essential conjunct. Co-delete this AND raw with a mismatched opposite split raw whose reader is outside the shadow (at most one of those three distinct OR readers is B). Thus three pure ORs plus B and the other raw's OR disappear, giving at most seven ANDs in the original. For each other split label keep it tied by choosing the co-deleted OR away from that label; two choices suffice (at most one is B). Saturated projection then forces every sole OR companion at the preserved label to be clean. At the AND raw's own label, its twin's OR companion cannot depend on that twin before its sole reader, and cannot accept the AND raw alone: its unique AND reader evaluates to zero on its singleton. Thus the twin is a clean feeder too. All seven OR feeders disappear; at most six terminal ORs are added, another contradiction.

If some split raw is repeated, both direct readers are OR: any direct AND would produce zero on deleting the raw and force a downstream collapse distinct from its two direct readers; a direct reader of the same primary cannot directly consume the AND result without absorption making a gate redundant. For a repeated left raw x, each of its three mismatched split right partners has a co-deletion shadow union of exactly three gates. A sole OR partner never shares its single gate with x. A two-gate shadow partner can intersect at most one reader of x (a sole AND's gate is not OR; two repeated OR readers with both raw operands equal would duplicate). Also at least one of the three partners must have a one-gate OR shadow: each of x's two ORs has just one other operand and can cover at most one two-gate shadow (either another repeated raw directly or the unique AND output of a sole-reader raw). Two same-side unique raws cannot feed the same sole AND, since swapping them changes the Crown table. This co-deletion removes six OR gates counting the three pairs, so there are at most six ANDs in the original. The symmetric statements hold if the repeated raw is right.

For any sole OR on a different label from a repeated raw, choose a co-deletion involving that repeated raw and avoiding the OR's label. Saturation of the removal budget forces the surviving OR's companion to reject its own diagonal. Suppose there are only one or two split raws that are not sole OR readers. With one repeated split raw and no AND reader, at least six sole OR feeders on other labels are clean. With two repeated split raws and no AND reader, all six sole OR feeders can be shown clean: if the repeated raws have the same label, the six lie elsewhere; if different, use a different repeated raw's label for each sole OR. With one repeat and one sole AND, all six sole ORs are clean as well: the only possible unchecked one lies at the repeated label, and if present use the sole AND raw at a different label and an opposite sole OR whose distinct reader is not its downstream consumer and whose label avoids the repeated label. Two opposite choices ensure one works. Again the six removals saturate. In all these cases at least six feeders disappear and at most five terminal ORs are added; this contradicts the three-pair size-21 exclusion.

These cases do not rule out every three-pair 22-gate footprint or any unrestricted raw circuit size 22. A three-pair candidate with no repeated raw needs at least two split AND readers; one with repeated raws needs at least three split raws that are not sole OR readers and at most six total ANDs. These are unconditional necessary restrictions within the stated architecture, not results of solver timeouts.
