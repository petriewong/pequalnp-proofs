# Two pure matched ORs at 22 gates require two exceptional split raws

An ordinary split raw has exactly one OR reader. Zero exceptions were excluded in CrownTwoPairsUniqueOr.md. Suppose there is a smallest useful monotone Crown7 circuit with exactly two pure matched ORs, at most 22 gates and only one exceptional split raw r. Use simplified binary gates (distinct operands and no constants); creating another pure pair during a simplification is covered by CrownNoThreePairs.md.

Let D be the elementary zero-shadow of r: propagate forced zeros through ANDs and all-zero ORs, and also count any OR with one forced-zero operand that aliases its other operand. For each cut through r and an opposite raw at a different split label, two pure gates collapse and the remaining projection computes seven-variable threshold two (gate floor 16, OR floor 10). Hence d = |D| is at most four. Also d >= 2: either r has distinct direct readers, or its sole AND reader is forced zero and a path to the unforced output has another lost gate.

The four compatible opposite ordinary feeders are distinct. None directly reads r: a sole OR reading both r and a mismatched opposite raw masks the pivot from r's diagonal to a superset containing a prescribed positive. Thus at least one gate in D is not one of these four feeders, leaving at least one opposite choice whose feeder is outside D. Its cut loses two pure ORs, the chosen feeder, and all ORs in D. If D contains one OR, there were at least 14 original ORs and at most eight ANDs; if D contains two ORs there were at least 15 original ORs and at most seven ANDs.

D contains at least one OR: if no OR directly reads r, follow a path from a forced-zero direct reader to the unforced output. Its first unforced gate is an OR that loses one operand. If any OR directly reads r, there are at least two ORs in D. This is immediate with two direct ORs. Otherwise the unique direct OR is G, and r also has at least one direct AND reader. If G were the only OR in D, all paths from all forced-zero gates to the output would exit the forced-zero region through G. On r=0 these gates are already all zero; on r=1, G=1 independent of the region. Therefore replacing all the forced gates by zero preserves the function. G then aliases r or remains unchanged; only G can be the first nonzero boundary. No ordinary feeder is among the removed gates: the only removed OR could be G, which cannot be a mismatched ordinary feeder by masking; a matched ordinary feeder G with operands r and its twin could not be the sole exit of any forced gate. Redirecting G to r cannot introduce a reader of any other raw, so the smaller resulting circuit still has exactly two pure pairs and at most one exceptional raw, or falls into an already excluded case. This contradicts minimality.

An ordinary feeder's companion can accept only its own diagonal by the pivot masking argument. There is at most one own-dirty feeder per label: its companion must read the twin before the feeder, so two own-dirty companions in a fully ordinary label require contradictory topological order. Suppose two different labels away from r both have own-dirty feeders. Cut through r and an opposite raw avoiding these two labels: there are two available opposite choices. If d=2, at most one of their feeders overlaps D, so take the other; if d>=3, D already supplies at least three losses. Both kept dirty feeders survive these raw deletions (their raw and their true diagonal companion are still present). Then both alias their companions after tying twins, since each companion is true on the retained singleton. This gives at least three elementary losses, two pure losses, and two distinct alias losses, exceeding six. Hence at most one feeder away from r's label is dirty.

If no direct OR reads r, the feeder at r's own label is clean too. Its companion cannot depend on that feeder's own raw, since it precedes the sole reader. On r alone every nonraw gate is false, by topological induction: every direct reader of r is AND with a distinct operand, and other gates receive no true raw. The companion is not r itself (which would be a direct OR reader). It therefore rejects the own diagonal as well.

Use clean erasure to build a terminalized circuit as in CrownNoThreePairs.md, keeping both original essential pure pairs. If r has no direct OR reader, remove at least eight clean-companion feeders and add at most seven ORs for the at-most-eight AND products. Otherwise remove at least seven clean-companion feeders and add at most six ORs for the at-most-seven AND products. Either way the terminalized circuit has at most 21 gates, excluded in CrownTwoPairsUniqueOr.md, or has at least three pure matched ORs, already excluded. The unrestricted 22-gate question remains open.
